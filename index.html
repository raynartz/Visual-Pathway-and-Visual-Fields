Interpretation

Teaching points and limitations

The retinal image is reversed horizontally and vertically. Temporal visual fields project to nasal retina; nasal visual fields project to temporal retina. Superior visual fields project to inferior retina and inferior visual fields to superior retina.

Nasal retinal fibers cross in the optic chiasm. A unilateral postchiasmal lesion therefore affects the contralateral visual field in both eyes. Inferior retinal fibers carrying the superior field travel through the temporal optic radiation; superior retinal fibers carrying the inferior field travel through the parietal radiation.

Lesions are idealized: complete optic nerve loss, selective central crossing-fiber loss, complete tract or LGN loss, and quadrant-specific radiation loss. Actual lesions may produce partial, irregular, or incongruous defects. LGN lesions can have patterns other than complete hemianopia.

Macular sparing is optional and is not universal in cortical lesions. Its illustrated size is arbitrary. Physiological blind spots, monocular field extent differences, the temporal crescent, and detailed cortical retinotopy are not modeled. Preserved central vision does not imply that all other visual functions are normal.

The pathway diagram traces hemifields rather than separate superior and inferior signal streams. The optic radiation branches are schematic. Moving dots indicate connectivity, not conduction speed.
